What waiting costs you today

Every change to your business software queues behind engineering, even a small one. On the way it is handed from one step to the next, and some of the meaning is lost each time.

What comes back is late, and not quite what was asked for. Your engineers spend their days translating and coordinating instead of engineering. In a regulated firm, the record of who asked, what was checked and who approved is rebuilt by hand before every audit.

AI has not fixed this. It made the coding faster. Everything around the code still waits.

How one change goes with Kogent

An example, not a customer

A finance operations lead has to raise an expense-approval limit and add one rule: nobody approves their own claim.

  1. The finance lead

    She writes the change in her own words.

  2. Kogent

    It drafts the checks that will show the change works, and asks her one question about a case she had not covered.

  3. AI agents

    They build the change and test it against those checks.

  4. The finance lead

    She reads, in plain words, what was checked and what was not.
    4 of 5 checks proven; 1 was never checked.

  5. A second person

    Someone else approves the release to production.

  6. The auditor

    Asks who approved it and what was tested. The answer is already on record.

What changes

Your experts deliver the change.
The people who know the business take it from idea to release themselves, with no ticket queue.
Engineers set the rules, and stop being the bottleneck.
Standards they confirm can go into every build. You choose which approvals need a person; production always needs one from someone else.
Everyone sees what was checked.
And what was not. Each result says so in plain words, so the person who asked can judge it.
The record builds itself.
Checks, reviews and approvals are recorded as the work happens. One click exports a release's evidence.
Spend and progress stay in view.
An owner can set a monthly limit on AI spend, and your whole product is on one page.
